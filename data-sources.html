Data sources and licences

Outbound combines open data from many public sources. Each is credited here with its licence and when the copy in this version of the app was pulled. Map packs you store list their own sources and build dates under Offline maps.

Maps, places and search

  • Natural Earth – country and state borders, highways, country names. Public domain. naturalearthdata.com. Pulled 2026-09-23.
  • GeoNames – cities of 15,000+ people (US, Canada, Mexico). Licence: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0). geonames.org. Pulled 2026-09-16.
  • Natural Earth states and provinces – which state or province each city is in. Public domain. Pulled 2026-09-24.
  • OpenStreetMap via Overture Maps – the built-in road network for the whole United States (motorways and highways, built into the app) and the street data downloaded around each end of a drive. (c) OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0; Overture Maps Foundation. Pulled 2026-09-27.
  • Apple Maps (iPhone, with a signal) – searching for businesses, addresses and landmarks uses Apple's MapKit place search. Results are shown, not stored, apart from the places you add to a trip.
  • Android's place search (Android, with a signal) – the phone's own geocoder (Google, through Android) finds businesses, addresses and landmarks. Results are shown, not stored, apart from the places you add to a trip.
  • Photon by komoot (when the phone's search finds nothing, and on other devices, with a signal) – place and address search over OpenStreetMap data. (c) OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. photon.komoot.io.

Exploring: collections, passport and discoveries

  • U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundaries 2023 (1:20,000,000) – the states and counties of the explored map and the collections. Public domain. census.gov. Pulled 2026-10-11.
  • National Park Service boundaries – the 63 national parks. Public domain. NPS Land Resources Division. Pulled 2026-10-11.
  • Wikidata – each state's highest point, its position and height. CC0. wikidata.org. Pulled 2026-10-11.
  • Wikipedia – the "You're passing..." notes and the discovery log: the first sentences of articles about places near the road, fetched while you drive or when a trip is kept offline. Text from Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia, under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence (CC BY-SA 4.0); each note links to its article.

Street map (streamed, kept on the phone)

  • OpenStreetMap – the street map in every style: streets and their names, highways, water, parks and woods, land use, buildings, railways, county and city lines. (c) OpenStreetMap contributors. Licence: Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0, openstreetmap.org/copyright.
  • OpenMapTiles – the vector tile layout the street map comes in. (c) OpenMapTiles, openmaptiles.org. Licence: BSD (code) / CC BY 4.0 (schema).
  • OpenFreeMap – serves those tiles, free and without an account, updated weekly. openfreemap.org. Tiles you have viewed are kept on your phone so the map also works offline there; the map drawn from them is made by Outbound.

World terrain (in the app)

  • NOAA ETOPO 2022 – the whole globe's land and sea-floor heights, 60 arc-second (~2 km), from which the app's built-in world terrain is made (the sea is shown at sea level). Public domain. NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information. Pulled 2026-09-26.
  • EGM96 geoid – converts heights to the WGS84 ellipsoid. National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency. Public domain.

Photo imagery (streamed)

  • The National Map, USGS Imagery Only – photo mode's imagery when you have internet: Blue Marble and Landsat views of the globe, and aerial photos across the United States (mostly the USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program, NAIP). Public domain. "Map services and data available from U.S. Geological Survey, National Geospatial Program." Capture dates vary by area; the service's imagery was last refreshed June 2024.

Offline map packs

  • USGS 3D Elevation Program (3DEP) – terrain heights, 1/3 arc-second (~10 m) in park packs and 1 arc-second (~30 m) in state terrain packs. Public domain. U.S. Geological Survey.
  • EGM96 geoid – converts heights to the WGS84 ellipsoid. National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency. Public domain.
  • USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program (NAIP) – aerial photos where a pack includes them. Public domain. The year of the photos is shown with each pack.
  • Shaded relief – where a pack has no photos, imagery is drawn from the USGS elevation data.
  • Overture Maps Foundation, transportation – road networks for offline routing in map packs, with one-way streets, speed limits and road surface. Built from OpenStreetMap: (c) OpenStreetMap contributors, Overture Maps Foundation. Licence: Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0. Release 2026-09-23, pulled 2026-09-26. The road database derived from it that ships in this app is itself available under the ODbL; contact us for a copy.
  • USGS National Transportation Dataset – trails outside the Sierra Nevada (and the road networks of packs built before 2026-09-26). Public domain. U.S. Geological Survey (from U.S. Census Bureau TIGER data). California file of 2026-02-12, pulled 2026-09-25.

Places, businesses and addresses (in map packs)

  • Overture Maps Foundation, places – businesses, hotels, campgrounds, gas, food, visitor centres and sights. (c) Overture Maps Foundation and its data sources (including Meta and Microsoft). Licences: Community Data License Agreement – Permissive 2.0, Apache 2.0 and CC0, as marked per record. overturemaps.org. Release 2026-09-23, pulled 2026-09-25. Opening hours, phone numbers and websites can be out of date: call ahead.
  • Overture Maps Foundation, addresses – street addresses, from county and state address data collected by OpenAddresses. Licence per source; in California, public records under state law. Release 2026-09-23, pulled 2026-09-25.
  • USGS Geographic Names Information System (GNIS) – peaks, lakes, waterfalls, springs, passes and other natural features. Public domain. U.S. Geological Survey. California file of 2026-08-28, pulled 2026-09-25. A spring on the map is not a promise of water: many run dry; treat any water you collect.

Trails

  • Overture Maps Foundation, transportation (paths and footways) – hiking trails in the downloadable park maps (Joshua Tree, Sequoia and Kings Canyon, Zion, Grand Canyon). Built from OpenStreetMap: (c) OpenStreetMap contributors, Overture Maps Foundation. Licence: ODbL 1.0. Release 2026-09-23, pulled 2026-09-26. Difficulty is worked out from USGS elevation data.
  • OpenStreetMap – hiking trails in the Sierra Nevada. (c) OpenStreetMap contributors. Licence: Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0, openstreetmap.org/copyright. Pulled 2026-09-24. The trail database derived from OpenStreetMap that ships in this app is itself available under the ODbL; contact us for a copy.
  • USGS National Transportation Dataset – trails elsewhere in California, from the U.S. Forest Service, National Park Service, Bureau of Land Management, California State Parks, California Coastal Commission and local agencies. Public domain. Pulled 2026-09-25.
  • Trail difficulty is calculated by Outbound from USGS elevation data; trailheads are the trail end nearest a road.

Weather and conditions (online)

  • U.S. National Weather Service (api.weather.gov) – forecasts and weather alerts. Public domain. A dated copy is kept on the phone for use without a signal.
  • AirNow (U.S. EPA and its state, local and tribal partners) – current air quality (AQI). AirNow data are preliminary and have not been fully verified; they are not the official record. Fetched through Outbound's own server, with your position rounded to about 10 km.
  • National Park Service – park alerts (closures, hazards, conditions). Public domain. Fetched through Outbound's own server by park code only.
  • Sunrise, sunset and twilight are worked out on the phone (NOAA's solar equations).

Health

  • Apple Health / Health Connect – your own steps and distance, read on your device only.
